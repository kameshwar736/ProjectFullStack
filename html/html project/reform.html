1. Teaching Methodology

From a college student’s perspective, the teaching methodology in many Indian colleges is still very traditional and mainly focused on lectures and note-taking. In most classes, professors explain the subject while students listen and copy notes. This method helps cover the syllabus but often limits student interaction, creativity, and critical thinking.
Students feel that teaching should be more interactive and engaging. Instead of only listening to lectures, students should participate in discussions, presentations, group activities, and problem-solving sessions. These methods help students understand concepts better and develop confidence in expressing their ideas.

  • More interactive classes instead of only lectures.
  • Use case studies, discussions, and presentations.
  • Encourage critical thinking rather than memorization.

2. Practical Learning

From a college student’s point of view, practical learning is one of the most important parts of education, but in many colleges in India it is still limited. Most subjects focus mainly on theoretical knowledge, while practical applications are often given less importance.
Students feel that colleges should provide more hands-on learning opportunities. This includes lab work, real-world projects, field work, and internships. When students work on practical tasks, they can better understand how the concepts they study in class are used in real situations.
For example, engineering and IT students can build applications, websites, or prototypes, while commerce and management students can work on business case studies or market analysis projects. These experiences help students develop problem-solving skills, technical ability, and confidence.

  • Increase lab work, practical sessions, and real projects.
  • Provide industry-based assignments.
  • Encourage prototype building and experiments.

3. Industry Exposure

From a college student’s perspective, industry exposure is very important for understanding how the professional world works. In many colleges, students mainly learn theory in classrooms but get very little chance to see how industries actually operate. Because of this, many students feel unprepared when they enter the job market after graduation. Students believe colleges should create strong connections with companies and industries. This can include internships, industry visits, guest lectures by professionals, and live projects from companies. Such experiences help students understand real workplace environments, current technologies, and industry expectations. Internships are especially important because they allow students to gain hands-on experience, learn professional skills, and build networks with industry experts. It also helps students discover which career path suits them best. Guest lectures and workshops from industry professionals can give students practical insights that are not always found in textbooks.

  • Mandatory internships for all students.
  • Industry visits and live project collaborations.
  • Guest lectures from industry professionals.

4. Research and Innovation

From a college student’s perspective, research and innovation should be an important part of college education, but in many institutions students rarely get opportunities to explore new ideas or conduct research. Most of the time, the focus is only on completing the syllabus and passing exams. Students believe colleges should encourage curiosity, experimentation, and creative thinking. Instead of only studying existing knowledge, students should also be motivated to discover new solutions, develop new technologies, and work on innovative ideas. Colleges can support this by providing research projects, innovation labs, and incubation centers where students can experiment and develop their ideas. Participation in hackathons, research competitions, and technical conferences can also motivate students to think creatively and solve real-world problems. Faculty guidance is also important. When professors mentor students in research activities, it helps them learn how to analyze problems, conduct studies, and present their findings.

  • Encourage student research projects.
  • Provide funding for innovative ideas.
  • Establish college incubation centers and startup support.

5. Examination System

From a college student’s perspective, the examination system in many Indian colleges is highly exam-centered. Most of the evaluation depends on final semester exams, where students are expected to write long answers based on memorization. Because of this, many students focus more on studying only for exams rather than truly understanding the subject. Students believe the examination system should be more balanced and continuous. Instead of depending mainly on one final exam, colleges can evaluate students through projects, assignments, presentations, quizzes, and practical work throughout the semester. This type of assessment helps measure a student’s overall understanding, creativity, and practical ability. Another concern for students is that the current system often rewards rote learning, where memorizing textbook content can lead to high marks. Students feel exams should test conceptual understanding, analytical thinking, and real-life problem-solving skills. Including open-book exams, case-study based questions, and application-oriented tests can make the evaluation process more meaningful and relevant to real-world situations.

  • Reduce dependency on final exams.
  • Evaluate practical knowledge and creativity.
  • Introduce continuous assessment (projects, quizzes, presentations).
