Student POV About College

From a student’s perspective, college life is often expected to be a place for learning, creativity, and personal growth. However, many students feel that the current college system focuses more on academic performance and discipline rather than real understanding and skill development. Teaching methods in some colleges still rely heavily on traditional lectures and note-taking, which can make learning less interactive and engaging. Instead of encouraging discussion, creativity, and practical exploration, students are often expected to memorize information mainly for exams. Another concern for students is the strong exam-oriented approach followed by many institutions. Internal assessments, model exams, assignments, and semester examinations create continuous pressure to score high marks. Because of this, students sometimes focus more on passing exams than actually understanding the subject. This pressure can reduce motivation and interest in learning. Strict rules and regulations are also commonly mentioned by students. Many colleges enforce strict dress codes, attendance requirements, and restrictions on mobile phones or other modern tools. While discipline is important for maintaining order, students sometimes feel that these rules limit their freedom and self-expression. The rigid structure can make the college environment feel more restrictive than encouraging. Limited practical exposure is another issue students talk about. Although colleges conduct laboratory sessions and projects, many of them follow fixed procedures where students simply complete experiments and records. This does not always help students gain real-world problem-solving skills or industry experience. As a result, students may feel unprepared when they enter the professional world.

List of problem:

  1. Outdated Teaching Methodology
  2. Exam-Oriented Education
  3. Strict Rules and Limited Freedom
  4. Limited Practical Exposure
  5. Pressure from Academics and Expectations
  6. Placement Challenges
  7. Lack of Student Voice

Struggles Faced by Students in College

Academic Pressure – Students face pressure from assignments, projects, internal tests, and semester exams.

Time Management Problems – Balancing classes, studying, extracurricular activities, and personal life can be difficult.

Financial Difficulties – Paying tuition fees, buying books, and managing daily expenses can be stressful for some students.

Homesickness – Students who move away from home may feel lonely and miss their family.

Difficulty in Understanding Subjects – Some students struggle to understand complex subjects or teaching methods.

Competition and Grade Pressure – Students often feel pressure to get high marks to secure good jobs or placements.

Social Adjustment – Making new friends and adapting to a new environment can be challenging.

Mental Stress and Anxiety – Continuous pressure from academics and future career concerns can affect mental health.

Limited Practical Exposure – Some colleges focus more on theory than practical learning.

Uncertainty About Career – Many students feel confused about choosing the right career path after graduation.

How a Fresher Sees College

When a student first joins college as a fresher, everything feels new and exciting. College looks like a place full of opportunities, freedom, and new experiences. Freshers often feel curious about the campus, classrooms, teachers, and the people they will meet. They expect college to be different from school, with more independence and chances to explore their interests.
At the same time, freshers may also feel nervous and uncertain because they are entering a completely new environment. They need to adjust to new teaching methods, meet new classmates, and understand the college rules and academic system. Slowly, as they spend more time in college, they start to feel comfortable, build friendships, and become more confident.

Common perspectives of freshers about college:

  • Excitement about a New Environment
  • Expectation of Freedom
  • Making New Friends
  • Interest in Clubs and Activities
  • Nervousness and Fear
  • Adjusting to New Teaching Methods
